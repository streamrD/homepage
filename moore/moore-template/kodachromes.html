Julia Ann Moore

Black River, Michigan, c. 1957

Detroit, MI, 1964

View from the front porch of 16547 Coyle, the house where the family lived from 19__to 19__. This new 1964 Chevy Impala was the family car for many years and the car my mother learned to drive in 1964.

Paul Alan Moore, c. 1955

In front of the house, 16547 Coyle, possibly on Easter Sunday. The Buick probably belonged to a family member visiting that day.

Helen (Moore) Toth, c. 1955

Helping out with the dishes at 16547 Coyle. Helen would have been visiting Jim, Emily, Jimmy, Barbara and Paul possibly on Easter Sunday, with her husband Bill. My mother, Barb, remembers that this cabinet door never seemed to shut and says one of her school papers was pinned to the bulletin board.

Paul Alan Moore, c. 1955

All dressed up and not to happy to be posing for the picture. Probably Easter Sunday. The first family TV in the background.

Barbara Florence Moore, c. 1958

The dress was a hand-me-down reluctantly given to my mother by her cousin Julie, who was two years older than she. My mother never got to take ballet lessons, but loved dressing up in this costume. My grandfather had a floodlight he set up in the living room to take pictures like this.

Julia Ann Moore, mid 1950's

This picture was taken in an apartment Julia shared with a friend, Grace Walburn, in Highland Park, MI near 6 Mile and Woodward. The family was visiting her that evening for dinner. A typical menu would have been lamb, mashed potatos, rutabaga, gravy & mint jelly for the lamb.

In the background is a photo of her son James and her first grandson, James & Emily's son Jimmy. Other items in the background, like the Toby mug, were typical decorations she liked.

James Edward Moore, c. 1958

This picture, like several he did, was a self-portrait taken with a timer and the camera set up on a tripod. He liked experimenting with the camera and reading up on the lastest gadgets and photographic techniques.

Barbara and Paul Moore with their cousin, Karen Beth Joseph, c. 1955

Girls played in dresses back then. These dresses were most likely made by Florence Joseph, the girls' grandmother. This photo was taken in James and Emily's bedroom in the house on Coyle.

James Edward Moore with his mother, Julia Ann, late 1950's

This photo was taken at Julia's house in Black River, MI.

Emily Marie Moore, c. 1953

This picture was taken at the home of Emily's brother Robert and his wife Betty. Like Emily's husband James, Bob was an avid photographer and may have taken this photo. Emily is wearing a black velvet stole she reserved for special occasions, an item her daughter Barbara always wanted to get her hands on for playing dress-up.

Emily would be embarrased to see that she is smoking in this photo. She quit for good after she became a Christian in 1954.

Betty Joseph, date unknown

Betty was the wife of Robert Joseph, Emily (Joseph) Moore's older brother. Tragically, both Betty and her husband died of cancer inthe late 1960's. They left behind two sons, Bobby and Warren, and a daughter, Karen.

Moore kids with older boys, Dyers Bay, Canada, c. 1956

Dyers Bay was a vacation spot the family visited occasionally. They rented a cottage on the shore with a woodburning stove for cooking. James would get up and start a fire with birchwood, and Emily baked apple pie.

Barbara is on far left, Jimmy on far right, and Paul is closest to the water in background.

James Edward Moore, c. 1953-4

Taken at the home of Robert Joseph on Red Leaf Lane in Southfield, MI. Photo may have been taken by Robert, James's brother-in-law, who was also an avid photographer. Robert had a darkroom in his basement.

Moore kids in dining room with Boston Coolers, c. 1954-5

This photo was taken on a hot summer evening shortly after the street lights came on, which meant it was time to come in from playing.

Boston Coolers are made from Vernor's (a ginger ale popular in Michigan) and ice cream.

James E. Moore cutting the turkey, Christmas, c. 1955

Taken at the home of Emily's sister, Marcia (Joseph) Clark, and her husband James on Denwood in Dearborn, MI.

Marcia and Jim, who didn't have children of their own, pampered Jim and Emily's kids with great presents. That year my mother Barbara received a large stuffed black French poodle (what every little girl wanted that year) and a little red leather overnight case.

James Vincent Moore, c. 1959-60

Taken at the house on Coyle. James had a rough life, which came to an untimely end in 1998 from years of drug abuse. Although he was extremely smart, he never adjusted well to school—maybe had ADD, but back then he was just thought to be badly behaved. As a teenager, he did start to get into real trouble, and the kind of friends he hung around didn't help. He worked sporadically as a printer and electrician and doing various other kinds of odd jobs. Because of all the problems, his relationship with his father, my grandfather, was always strained. Jimmy was his firstborn, named after his brother Vince who died during the Normandy Invasion. My grandfather made it through the war, fighting in the Pacific. Things weren't supposed to turn out like this after such an ordeal.

I remember him as a fantastic uncle. He had an active, creative mind and was always good at explaining how things worked. In the late '70s and early '80s he could build his own computer.
